Multi-Waste Polyphenol Bio-Composite Mat

A student-designed concept exploring the reuse of date-palm, tea and coffee waste in a porous composite for environmental applications.

Interactive project display

01 — Experimental observations

Blue food-colouring water was passed through two sand setups. The observations below are qualitative.

Control

Bare sand

Dark-blue effluent observed

The collected water appeared strongly blue.

Experimental setup

Sand + tile

Almost-clear effluent observed

The collected water appeared much less blue.

Interpretation: The experimental setup showed a visible colour difference. Because the tile and sand were tested together, this observation does not isolate the tile's effect.

This is a qualitative comparison, not a numerical graph. The colour swatches illustrate the reported appearance and are not measured data. Food colouring is not a heavy metal; metal immobilisation requires separate metal-specific testing.

03 — What's inside the composite?

The diagram below represents the finished tile from above. The ingredient list identifies the materials used in the prototype.

Date-palm leaves
Processed plant fibres forming the main scaffold.
Spent coffee grounds
Fine waste material incorporated into the mixture.
Black tea waste
Contains plant compounds, including polyphenols.
Cornstarch binder
Helps hold the composite together.

Baking soda was used during preparation as a pretreatment. It is not shown as a main structural ingredient in the finished tile.

04 — Next steps for further research

  • Repeat the experiment with multiple trials.
  • Record effluent colour using a consistent method.
  • Compare sand-only and tile-containing setups under the same conditions.
  • Use an appropriate laboratory method to test actual heavy-metal concentrations before making claims about metal removal.
